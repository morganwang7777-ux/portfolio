IMD test box

UW Formula Electric · Sep – Oct 2026 · Altium Designer, safety analysis, high-voltage electronics

View on GitHub

What: Developing a passive insulation monitoring fault injection test box for the Formula Electric vehicle, interfacing through the HV+/HV- Tractive System Measuring Points (TSMP) and chassis reference for controlled test conditions.

How: Created Altium Designer schematics and resistor networks. Accounted for 15 kOhm TSMP protection resistance and evaluated a 588 V tractive system against the FSAE 250 Ohm/V insulation criterion.

Result: Calculated a 147 kOhm critical insulation threshold and design target equivalent fault resistances of 285, 485, and 75 kOhm. Planned selection states include PASS, WARNING, TECH FAIL, and a hard short scenario. The design provides a repeatable method to investigate firmware reported warnings and vehicle shutdown responses.

Next steps: Physical testing on the car after the PCB has been printed.

5 V DC-DC buck converter

UW Midnight Sun design task · Sep – Oct 2026 · LTspice, Altium Designer, PCB design

View on GitHub

What: Designed a compact 10-15 V input to regulated 5 V, 1 A switching buck converter using the LT1375 for a low voltage power supply design task.

How: Selected a 15 uH inductor, Schottky freewheeling diode, input/output capacitors, and resistive feedback components using datasheets and calculations. Simulated switching behavior and component trade offs in LTspice.

Result: Reduced simulated output ripple from 45.7 mVpp (v1) to 23.6 mVpp (48.3%, v2) by changing output capacitor ESR from 0.10 to 0.05 Ohm and tuning compensation (1 kOhm / 3.3 nF) at 15 V input and 1 A load.

PCB: Designed a 41.4 x 23.6 mm two layer SMD layout in Altium Designer with 25 mil power traces, 10 mil signal traces, a bottom layer GND polygon, and via connected returns. Reported zero violations under the DRC rules.

Design specifications

Goal: an asynchronous buck converter that takes 10–15 V in and outputs 5 V at 1 A, using surface mount parts only. Assumes 90% efficiency, tested at 15 V.

From v1 to v2

V1 used a 15 uH inductor, a 100 uF output capacitor with 0.10 ohm modeled ESR, and a 3.3 nF VC compensation capacitor.

For V2, I decreased R4 from 0.1 Ω to 0.05 Ω to reduce the output capacitor's ESR. Since the capacitor handles changing current when the buck converter switches, having less resistance means less voltage fluctuation and therefore lower ripple. I also added a 1 kΩ resistor to the compensation circuit to help control how the IC responds to voltage changes and prevent oscillations.

The independent effect of each modification has not yet been fully isolated.

PCB layout and design rule check

Component placement: Grouped the LT1375, input capacitor, Schottky diode, BOOST circuitry, and inductor to reduce switching-path lengths.

Power routing: The DigiKey trace width calculator gives a 20.7 mil minimum for 1.5 A on an external 1 oz layer (10 °C rise, 40 °C ambient), so major power connections use 25 mil traces. Feedback and compensation signals use about 10 mil.

Ground plane: Created a Bottom Layer GND polygon pour and connected ground pads using short traces and vias.

DRC: 0 violations across 14 Altium rules, including 1 mil clearance, short-circuit, un-routed nets, 10–30 mil trace width, 10 mil hole-to-hole clearance and 6 mil solder mask sliver.

Next steps

  • Validate realistic capacitor and inductor characteristics across operating conditions.
  • Manufacture, assemble, and electrically test the PCB.

Morgan's Metronome v1.0

Personal project · Jul – Sep 2026 · Arduino UNO, C++, KiCad, embedded systems

View on GitHub

What: Built an Arduino UNO based digital metronome after finding myself getting distracted using my phone while practicing saxophone. Designed it to help maintain a consistent rhythm, featuring adjustable 40–180 BPM tempo, selectable beat patterns, pushbutton controls, a potentiometer, and a 16×2 LCD.

How: Programmed the timing and user interface in C++. Used millis() based non blocking timing to schedule beats while keeping tempo and button controls responsive. Prototyped and debugged the circuit on a breadboard before designing a custom Arduino UNO shield PCB in KiCad to integrate the components.

Result: Developed a functional metronome with an audible buzzer and visual LED beat indicators, adjustable tempo, and selectable rhythm patterns. PCB is on its way!

Enclosure: SolidWorks enclosure designed by Jordan Lin.
